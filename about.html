About Me

I’m a postdoctoral associate in the Mims Lab at Virginia Tech, where I work on landscape genetics and conservation of amphibians.

I earned my Ph.D. in 2026 from the Bredesen Center for Interdisciplinary Research and Graduate Education at the University of Tennessee, Knoxville, in Energy Science and Engineering with a concentration in Environmental and Climate Sciences. My B.S. there, in Ecology and Evolutionary Biology, came in 2020.

Before Virginia Tech, I worked with Oak Ridge National Laboratory on connectivity and conservation planning, and I co-led the Clinch River Environmental Studies Organization, a student research program in Oak Ridge, Tennessee.

Mentoring and teaching

I’ve mentored undergraduate students since 2022, several of whom are coauthors on published work, and I serve on an MS thesis committee. I’ve given guest lectures at North Carolina State University and Johnson University.

Service

I review manuscripts for journals including Herpetological Conservation and Biology, PeerJ, and Journal for Nature Conservation, and I lead public hikes and outreach programs in the Great Smoky Mountains and Oak Ridge.
